# Arabic glossary (ar)

Modern Standard Arabic, the plain kind used in popular consumer apps. Written right to left (`"dir": "rtl"`).

## Register

- Direct "you" (أنت), the way Arabic apps talk to people. No formal titles like حضرتك.
- Arabic "you" has a gender. Like most Arabic apps, we use the masculine form as the default for an unknown reader (تريد، افتح، قل). Where it's easy, sentences avoid gender altogether (ما اسمك؟).
- {Name} is referred to by name where possible. When a pronoun is needed, it's masculine by default (يريد، يقول). Your agent should switch to the feminine form once it knows the person is a woman.
- Digits: Western digits (0-9), as in most apps, so rule numbers, times and dates match the info blocks.
- Plain words, short sentences. No heavy classical phrasing.

## Review decisions

The AI review kept three terms the translator was unsure about: briefs = المتطلبات (what the client asks for, and "موجز" stays for the morning brief), prompts = الأوامر (with "(prompts)" after it in sentences), your agent = مساعدك الذكي (short form مساعدك).

## Example names

Ana Costa becomes سارة خليل (sara.draws, sarakhalil, sarasketchbook), Marta becomes مريم, João becomes عمر, Lia becomes ليلى, Lisbon, Portugal becomes عمّان، الأردن. "Admin on Fridays" becomes Thursdays (الخميس), since Friday is the weekend in most Arab countries.

## Trigger phrases

Each Arabic phrase comes first, then the English one, so both work: "افحص عقلي"، "check up my brain".
